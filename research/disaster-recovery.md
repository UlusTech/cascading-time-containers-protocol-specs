## 1. Phases and "build back better"

- Common framing: relief (days to weeks) → early recovery (weeks to months) → reconstruction (months to years) → long-term development. The phases overlap and differ by sector and by place. `[memory-high]` The SBB report frames Türkiye's own plan in the same order: emergency and temporary shelter, then a focus on permanent housing "in the coming one-year period" `[sourced: SB p.39-40]`.
- "Build back better" was named in the UN Sendai Framework (2015) as a priority for recovery, after use in the 2004 tsunami response (Clinton envoy). Criticised as a slogan that pushes speed and quality against each other. `[memory-high]`
- PDNA (Post-Disaster Needs Assessment): a joint method of the EU, UN and World Bank (GFDRR hosts the guidance), started at the request of the government; estimates damage, losses and needs per sector and yields a recovery strategy costed by time horizon. `[memory-high]` The exact time window in the current guidance `[memory-low]`.
- Turkish estimate for 2023: the total burden of the earthquakes on the economy is put at about 2 trillion TL, US$ 103.6 billion, about 9 percent of 2023 national income `[sourced: SB p.10]`. **Correction:** the memory version attributed this to a rapid assessment by the Strategy and Budget Presidency with UNDP, EU and World Bank and called it "damage and losses". The SBB report is the Presidency's own report; it counts the figure as a "total burden" that includes insurance-sector losses, tradesmen's income losses and macroeconomic effects `[corrected: SB p.10]`. Its housing table gives housing damage of 1,145.3 billion TL (US$ 60.7 billion) and losses of 101.8 billion TL (US$ 5.3 billion) `[sourced: SB p.39]`.
- World Bank figure of about US$ 34 billion of direct physical damage `[memory-low]` (no local source; the local Wikipedia copy only has the World Bank number for Syria).
- Debris: UNDP estimated 116 to 210 million tons of debris in Türkiye; building debris about 100 million m3, about ten times the 2010 Haiti earthquake `[sourced: AF]`.
- Inference, not a claim: the PDNA-type number is an estimate that is later replaced by real spending; a plan drawn from it is a prediction that changes.

## 2. Housing: the shelter continuum

- Sequence commonly used by the Shelter Cluster: emergency shelter (tents, tarpaulins, host families) → temporary/transitional shelter (prefab, containers, t-shelters) → permanent housing. Not everyone passes each step. `[memory-high]` In Türkiye 2023 the state also used dormitories, guest houses, hotels, teacher houses, summer houses and vineyard houses as temporary shelter `[sourced: SB p.32]`.
- Design intent: emergency shelter days to a few months; transitional shelter up to 2–3 years. `[memory-low]` The SBB report states the Turkish principle that tents and containers as short-term shelter should not exceed 6 months and that people should reach permanent homes as soon as possible `[sourced: SB p.39]`. FEMA policy allowed 18 months in a trailer from the day of access `[sourced: FT]`.
- Reality: "temporary" often lasts far longer than planned.
  - FEMA trailers after Katrina: policy 18 months, extended so that people could stay up to 45 months; in May 2009 FEMA sent eviction notices to Mississippi residents, then extended a deadline on 30 May; the temporary program formally ended in 2009 with about 3,400 people still in trailers `[sourced: FT; DK]`.
  - Haiti: about 1.5 million in camps after the quake; 370,000 in tent camps in October 2012; 279,000 in 352 camps in 2013; about 55,000 in camps and makeshift camps in 2017 `[sourced: HA]`.
  - Tōhoku: temporary housing designed for two years, extended repeatedly, last units closed around 2020–2021 `[memory-low]`. What the source gives is the early scale: 46,081 temporary units built by the end of July 2011, about 88 percent of those planned, of which 73 percent were occupied `[sourced: TA]`.
  - İzmit 1999: prefab settlements used for years, some into the 2000s `[memory-low]`. The 2010 parliamentary research report counts 40,786 prefabricated homes distributed and 147,120 people rehoused into them `[sourced: IZ]`.
- Türkiye 2023:
  - Large tent phase: in early March 2023, 332 tent cities and 360,167 tents sheltered 1,440,668 people; 1,593,808 were sheltered in the region and 329,960 people affected were sheltered in other provinces; over 1.9 million people were rehoused in dormitories, guest houses, tents, hotels and containers `[sourced: SB p.32; AF]`.
  - Container cities: 189 container cities and 90,914 containers were being set up, with 34,120 people in containers, as of early March 2023 `[sourced: SB p.32]`. One year later about 689,000 people were still living in container homes, generally 21 m2 `[sourced: AF]`.
  - Permanent housing target. **Correction:** the memory version said "about 300,000 in one year, later about 453,000". The sources give other numbers: on 9–10 February President Erdoğan promised to rebuild destroyed homes within one year `[sourced: AF]`; by 3 March 2023 the SBB report lists 405,505 planned homes plus 83,149 village houses, about 608 billion TL of construction cost for the first group `[corrected: SB p.40]`; on 22 April Erdoğan said 507,000 houses and 143,000 village homes were being built, that construction had begun on 105,000, and that 319,000 were planned for completion by the end of 2023 `[corrected: AF]`. I found no source for 300,000 or 453,000. The target figure moved between these dates, which is itself the observation.
  - TOKİ and AFAD build: AFAD is legally responsible for permanent disaster housing and works by tender and through TOKİ; TOKİ aimed to lay foundations in 11 provinces in March 2023, with 30 billion TL allocated for a first 100,000 homes `[sourced: SB p.40-41]`.
  - First big handover late 2023 and mass handovers ("anahtar teslim") in 2024 and after; actual delivered counts on each date `[memory-low]`.
- Container city rules: households moved in by registration, sizes by household, moved when a bigger unit freed or a permanent home was delivered `[memory-low]`. The SBB plan asks that shelter sites preserve family unity `[sourced: SB p.41]`.

## 3. Reconstruction and what delays it

Delay sources (general): debris removal and disposal (asbestos, mass of material, searches for the missing); land and cadastral records (destroyed or contested, informal or inherited ownership, missing heirs); zoning and re-planning of ground (liquefaction, fault zones, "riskli alan" declarations); permits; contractor capacity and material price rises; financing; reconciliation of owners' wishes. `[memory-high]` The SBB plan confirms one link of the chain: geological study, micro-zoning and soil surveys had to finish before foundations could be laid `[sourced: SB p.40]`.

Türkiye specifics:
- Law 6306 (2012) on transformation of areas under disaster risk is the frame for risky areas and buildings `[sourced: SB p.24]`.
- Damage classes: urgent-demolition, destroyed and heavily damaged (518,009 homes together), moderately damaged (131,577 homes), lightly damaged (1,279,727 homes); 2,273,551 people faced a direct housing problem `[sourced: SB p.38]`.
- **Correction on legal basis:** the memory version tied hak sahipliği (rights-holder status) to Law 6306. The SBB report says rights arise under Law 7269 (aid measures after disasters affecting general life) for homes, workplaces and barns; lightly damaged homes give no right, only the 10,000 TL household payment. Law 6306 appears for rent, moving and furniture aid to rights-holders in risk areas `[corrected: SB p.33, p.39]`.
- Application by e-Devlet, allocation by lottery (kura) in the same province, "yerinde dönüşüm" versus a new home elsewhere, tenant rules `[memory-low]` (SBB does not cover these).
- Payment terms: for rights-holders TOKİ homes are offered at a significant discount below cost, with a 2-year grace and a 20-year term; the ready-home credit for heavily damaged homes and barns is interest-free with 2-year grace and 18 equal years, for workplaces 2-year grace and 8 years at 4 percent, for moderately damaged homes 2-year grace and 8 years interest-free `[sourced: SB p.40-41]`. **Note:** memory said "low or zero interest"; that holds for homes, workplaces carry 4 percent.
- Public-facing information sits with the Ministry of Environment, Urbanisation and Climate Change (csb.gov.tr) and AFAD (afad.gov.tr); not read.

## 4. Money and papers

### Replacing documents
- Identity cards and passports: replacements from the population directorate; in 2023 fee waivers and mobile offices were announced. `[memory-low]`
- Title deeds: the land registry keeps records centrally (TAKBİS) with backups, so many deeds could be reproduced; in Haiti and informal areas records were weak or missing. `[memory-low]`
- Diplomas: universities re-issue; school records via e-Okul (MEB) and YÖK. `[memory-low]` Some universities were themselves damaged (SBB has a damage table for university buildings) `[sourced: SB p.45]`.
- Medical records: e-Nabız keeps a personal health record online in Türkiye. Katrina paper charts drowned and pushed US electronic prescription histories. `[memory-low]`

### Insurance
- DASK: the Turkish Catastrophe Insurance Pool was created by Decree 587, in force September 2000, to run compulsory earthquake insurance for dwellings; Law 6305 (2012) made it a law and added fire, explosion, tsunami and landslide after earthquakes; village buildings are outside compulsory cover `[sourced: SB p.24; IZ]`. Coverage limited to the building up to a legal cap and not contents `[memory-low]`; claim notice deadlines and payment target `[memory-low]`.
- **Correction on 2023 payouts:** the memory version said payouts of "tens of thousands of claims". The SBB report gives 1,143,249 DASK policies in the 11 provinces; by early March 2023, 326,895 damage notices (82 percent classed light) and 2.0 billion TL paid `[corrected: SB p.34]`. A large share of buildings uninsured `[memory-low]`.
- US: flood insurance claim rules (NFIP proof of loss) and litigation over wind versus flood after Katrina. `[memory-low]`

### Government aid
- Türkiye 2023: 10,000 TL cash per household with a damaged home (estimated 19.3 billion TL in total); 15,000 TL moving aid for households with destroyed, urgent-demolition, heavily or moderately damaged homes; one year of monthly rent aid; 100,000 TL for relatives of the dead `[sourced: SB p.33, p.39]`. **Note on a source conflict:** the SBB report gives rent aid of 5,000 TL for owners and 3,000 TL for tenants; the announcement on 10 February (as given in both Wikipedia copies) gave 5,000 and 2,000 TL `[sourced: SB p.33; AF; TRD]`. Applications through AFAD and e-Devlet `[memory-low]`.
- US Katrina: "Road Home" program (Louisiana) launched in 2006; grants capped, long waits. `[memory-low]`

## 5. Work, school, debt

- Employment: in Türkiye a 3-month short-work allowance (March–May 2023) with cash wage support, a 6-month social-security premium deferral (March–August), and a community-benefit programme with 22,100 places, costing 13.3 billion TL together `[sourced: SB p.111-112]`. Unemployment insurance and a state support for those on leave `[memory-low]`.
- Force majeure: force majeure was declared on 9 February 2023 and tax obligations falling between 6 February and 31 July 2023 were postponed to 31 July 2023 `[sourced: AF]`. Legal basis in VUK art. 13; decisions of GİB and the President; further extensions in stages; a stay on enforcement proceedings; BDDK loan deferral and credit-card relief `[memory-low]`. The SBB report only lists loan relief as a recommendation: a 1-year debt deferral for affected SMEs and a 1-year extension of the grace period on investment loans `[sourced: SB p.101]` (a proposal, not proof it was enacted in that form). The general pattern, that when relief ends the debt is due whether or not income has returned, is an inference.
- Displaced schooling: YÖK decided that higher education would start remotely and reassessed a blended model from April; the SBB plan recommends directing pupils to the nearest safe school, double-shift teaching, full-day education, free meals and transport, and container classrooms with travelling teachers `[sourced: SB p.47, p.50]` (recommendations, not results). Nearly 250,000 displaced persons were living in schools across Malatya province `[sourced: AF]`. Classes moved to TV and EBA, "misafir öğrenci" (guest student) registration in other provinces `[memory-low]`.
- Katrina: host schools enrolled evacuee children quickly (Texas offered faster enrolment) `[sourced: DK]`. "Tens of thousands" of students and the rebuilt mostly-charter New Orleans system `[memory-low]`.

## 6. Health

- Psychosocial support: acute stress in weeks; a proportion develops PTSD, depression, or prolonged grief; support in the four IASC tiers. `[memory-high]` For scale in Türkiye: 3,984 psychosocial support staff in the region (7,810 including provinces outside it) and 1,220,744 interviews by early March 2023 `[sourced: SB p.111]`.
- Anniversary effects: symptoms rise around anniversaries and triggers. `[memory-low]`
- Prevalence figures for 2023 studies: not available. Gap.
- Chronic illness: dialysis, insulin, cancer therapy and mental-health drugs are interrupted when clinics close; in 2023 dialysis patients were relocated and crush-syndrome survivors needed dialysis; prescriptions were filled without the usual limits in any pharmacy. `[memory-low]`
- Tōhoku: "earthquake-related deaths" for the relief fund include fatigue from life in temporary shelter and evacuation, and delayed treatment because a hospital was down; most occurred within the first six months `[sourced: TO]`.

## 7. Communities, movement, return

- Many leave; some come back; some never do. `[memory-high]` New Orleans: 484,674 in April 2000, an estimated 230,172 in July 2006 (over half lost), and an estimated 383,997 in 2020, still below the pre-Katrina level `[sourced: DK]`. **Correction:** memory said "about 390,000 by 2019"; the source gives 383,997 as a 2020 estimate `[corrected: DK]`. Brookings put the city at about 72 percent of its pre-Katrina population (about 316,000) in 2008 `[sourced: DK]`. About 40 percent of evacuees from Louisiana did not return home `[sourced: DK]`.
- Tōhoku: 140,000 residents within 20 km of Fukushima I were evacuated `[sourced: TA]`. Evacuation orders lifted in stages from 2014 into the 2020s, some zones restricted, many evacuees settled elsewhere `[memory-low]`. Rikuzentakata: 80 percent of residential areas destroyed, land raised by 10 m and a 12.5 m seawall built; still rebuilding in 2021 `[sourced: TA]`.
- Hatay and other provinces in 2023: a significant part of the population left; the old centre of Antakya was largely destroyed; a debate on rebuilding in place versus new sites. `[memory-low]`
- Memorials and anniversaries: the first shock was at 04:17 Türkiye time on 6 February 2023 (the second at 13:24) `[sourced: SB p.8; TRD; AF]`. In Japan the earthquake was at 14:46 on 11 March `[sourced: TO]`. Dates fix a shared calendar for the whole country (inference).

## Observations relevant to CTCP's premise

These are observations, not design. Where a number appears it is tagged as above.

- **Nesting.** A single household appears under many overlapping plans at once: the shelter place, the rights-holder application, the child's school, the debt relief, the treatment schedule. One event (a home delivered) sits inside several of them.
- **Nesting, many parents.** A person is in one province's calendar and another's (guest student, relocated worker, family split across two cities). 329,960 people affected were sheltered outside the region in March 2023 `[sourced: SB p.32]`.
- **Pushing.** Delays chain: debris → land records → permit → contractor → handover → the move-in date → the school year → the loan deferral end. A promised move-in date slips repeatedly. FEMA's 18 months became 45 months for Katrina `[sourced: FT]`; Tōhoku's two years of "temporary" housing running to nine or more `[memory-low]`.
- **Order without time.** Many recovery steps have a fixed order and no honest date: "after the lottery", "after the deed is reissued", "after the new home is delivered". Officials give dates that later change: the one-year rebuild promise of 9–10 February 2023 sits beside 319,000 homes planned for the end of 2023 `[sourced: AF]`.
- **Kinds of time.** The damage estimate, the housing target, the return of income, all start as estimates and turn into measured facts. The 2023 housing target moved between 405,505 (3 March) and 507,000 (22 April) `[sourced: SB p.40; AF]`. Relief periods are exact dates set by decision (tax dates to 31 July 2023 `[sourced: AF]`), while the recovery they wait for is a range.
- **Conditions.** Rules are conditional and the conditions change: a deferral that ends before income returns; an insurance deadline that passed while the person was in a tent; a business that can reopen only if the landlord's building is rebuilt; a diploma needed for a job but lost with the house. (Examples, not sourced claims.)
- **Servers.** Several bodies (state, municipality, insurer, bank, employer, school, hospital, family) hold parts of one person's story, and privacy of damage and aid records is a live concern: each may need a fact without holding the rest. Anniversaries are a shared calendar that is also very private.
- **Long time scales.** Recovery lasts years; a plan made in month 1 will be read in year 8, by which time people, addresses and rules have changed. Rikuzentakata was still rebuilding ten years on `[sourced: TA]`.
