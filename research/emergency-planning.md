## Disaster management cycle

- Emergency management is generally categorised into preparedness, response, mitigation and recovery, "although other terms such as disaster risk reduction and prevention are also common". [src: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergency_management] **Corrected:** the draft said "four phases". The same page also states "five phases: prevention, mitigation, preparedness, response and recovery", so the count depends on the author. Mitigation (reduce hazard or exposure beforehand, for example earthquake retrofits) and preparedness (equipment and procedures ready for use) are as the draft said. [src: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergency_management]
- FEMA's model is a circle from mitigation to preparedness to response to recovery and back to mitigation, "circular, overlapping". [src: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergency_management] (the draft's "commonly drawn as a loop, phases overlap")
- Sendai's Priority 4 ties recovery back to risk reduction: "build back better, including through integrating disaster risk reduction into development measures". [src: https://www.undrr.org/implementing-sendai-framework/what-sendai-framework]

## Sendai Framework 2015–2030

- Adopted 18 March 2015, following the Third UN World Conference on Disaster Risk Reduction; successor to the Hyogo Framework for Action 2005–2015. [src: https://www.undrr.org/implementing-sendai-framework/what-sendai-framework] (Hyogo's adoption in January 2005 is also in https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergency_management.)
- Four priorities: (1) understanding disaster risk; (2) strengthening disaster risk governance; (3) investing in DRR for resilience; (4) enhancing disaster preparedness for effective response and to "Build Back Better". [src: https://www.undrr.org/implementing-sendai-framework/what-sendai-framework]
- Seven global targets, measured by 38 global indicators. Their subjects: reduce disaster mortality, people affected, economic loss and damage to infrastructure and basic services; increase the number of countries with DRR strategies, international aid to developing countries, and access to warning systems. [src: https://www.undrr.org/implementing-sendai-framework/what-sendai-framework for "seven targets, 38 indicators"; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disaster_risk_reduction for the subject list] Targets A to D are the loss-data targets (DesInventar Sendai tool) [src: UNDRR page].
- `[memory]` The letter order A–G as listed in the draft, the target years (2020 for target E, 2030 for the rest) and the 2020–2030 vs 2005–2015 comparison periods for A–D. The UNDRR page read says only "seven targets" and does not list them. Check the full target text at https://www.undrr.org/implementing-sendai-framework/sendai-framework-action-targets (not read).
- Extra fact: the number of countries with national DRR strategies rose from 55 (2015) to 123 (2022). [src: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disaster_risk_reduction (wiki)]

## Türkiye institutions

- AFAD (Afet ve Acil Durum Yönetimi Başkanlığı) was established by Law 5902 (dated 29 May 2009, published 17 June 2009), replacing the Interior Ministry's Civil Defence General Directorate, the Public Works Ministry's Disaster Affairs General Directorate and the Prime Ministry's Emergency Management General Directorate. [src: https://tr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afet_ve_Acil_Durum_Y%C3%B6netimi_Ba%C5%9Fkanl%C4%B1%C4%9F%C4%B1; https://www.sbb.gov.tr/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/2023-Kahramanmaras-ve-Hatay-Depremleri-Raporu.pdf [page 22]] It reports to the Interior Ministry; the SBB report says it was created under the Prime Ministry and was attached to the Interior Ministry by Presidential Decree No. 4 in 2018. [src: SBB [page 22]–[page 23]] Note: the Turkish Wikipedia says it was created under the Interior Ministry; the SBB account is the more careful one and is used here. The draft's "sits under the Interior Ministry" is correct for today.
- Türkiye Afet Müdahale Planı (TAMP), the national response plan, defines the roles and responsibilities of the disaster groups (afet grupları) and coordination units. It covers ministries, agencies, the private sector, NGOs and individuals. [src: https://www.afad.gov.tr/turkiye-afet-mudahale-plani] It has 25 disaster groups at national level and 23 at local level. Ten main solution partners are named (Presidency Communications Directorate, Interior, Family and Social Services, Environment/Urbanisation/Climate, Energy, Treasury and Finance, Health, Agriculture and Forestry, Transport and Infrastructure, and Kızılay); other bodies are support partners. [src: same page]
  - `[memory]` The draft's example group names (logistics, communication, health, search and rescue, shelter) are not confirmed: the page read names the lead institutions, not the 25 groups. Check the TAMP PDF linked from the page (3054 KB, not downloaded).
- **Corrected (dates):** the draft said the plan was "issued around 2014 and replaced by a regulation-based version around 2022". TAMP was first published in the Resmî Gazete on 3 January 2014 (No. 28871). It was revised and re-published on 15 September 2022 (No. 31954). The legal basis is a separate regulation, the Afet ve Acil Durum Müdahale Hizmetleri Yönetmeliği, published 24 February 2022 (No. 31760). So TAMP was not replaced by a regulation; it was revised, and a regulation stands behind it. [src: https://www.afad.gov.tr/turkiye-afet-mudahale-plani] (SBB [page 133] independently says TAMP came into force in 2014.)
- Provincial plans: `[memory]` that provincial "İl Afet Müdahale Planı" derive from TAMP and are owned by the governorates. What the sources do say: local coordination runs through the İl Afet ve Acil Durum Koordinasyon Kurulu, chaired by the governor, which meets at the provincial AFAD centre without waiting for a call in a disaster. [src: https://www.afad.gov.tr/turkiye-afet-mudahale-plani] The SBB report separately names 81 provincial "İl Risk Azaltma Planları" (risk reduction, not response). [src: SBB [page 133]]

## DASK

- DASK (Doğal Afet Sigortaları Kurumu) manages the compulsory earthquake insurance (Zorunlu Deprem Sigortası, ZDS), raised after the 17 August 1999 earthquake. [src: https://tr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Do%C4%9Fal_Afet_Sigortalar%C4%B1_Kurumu; SBB [page 24]]
- Decree-law (KHK) 587 was published on 27 December 1999 (Resmî Gazete 23919 mükerrer), DASK issued its first policy on 27 September 2000, and the SBB report says the decree came into force in September 2000. [src: https://tr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Do%C4%9Fal_Afet_Sigortalar%C4%B1_Kurumu; SBB [page 24]] The draft's "compulsory from around 2000 (Decree 587)" is consistent; the dates are now exact. (wiki for the gazette date)
- Law 6305 (Afet Sigortaları Kanunu) came into force in 2012 and made ZDS statutory. It added cover for fire, explosion, tsunami and landslide damage that follows an earthquake. [src: SBB [page 24]]
- **Corrected (scope):** the draft said "residential buildings in registered urban areas, not contents". The SBB report says ZDS covers land-registered, privately owned buildings built as dwellings; independent units under the Condominium Law (Kat Mülkiyeti Kanunu); commercial and office units inside such buildings; and disaster-relief housing built by the state or with state credit. It is designed for dwellings within municipal boundaries; buildings in villages and hamlets registered to the village population are not covered. It insures "bina zararı" (building damage). [src: SBB [page 24]] So the scope is wider than "residential" (commercial units in residential buildings are in) and the boundary is the municipal limit, not "registered urban areas". `[memory]` that contents are excluded: the source speaks only of building damage.
- `[memory]` That the policy is annual and renewed every year. The pages read show a "Poliçe Sorgulama" (policy query) function but do not state the term. Check https://www.dask.gov.tr (ZDS Genel Şartlar). This claim underlies several cases.
- The coverage cap: DASK announced on 3 January 2024 that "ZDS Tarife ve Teminatları Güncellendi", that is, the cover amounts for dwellings were updated. [src: https://www.dask.gov.tr/] So the draft's "raised over time" is supported; the current figure was not read and is still unknown here.
- Link to official transactions: the DASK site lists "Kontrol Noktaları" (checkpoints) for "Konut Kredileri ve Tapu İşlemleri", "Elektrik Aboneliği" and "Su Aboneliği". [src: https://www.dask.gov.tr/] So a valid policy is checked at mortgage lending, title-deed transactions and electricity and water subscriptions. `[memory]` that enforcement was historically weak. The SBB report does say tracking of the disaster insurance declaration and payment must be made effective. [src: SBB [page 137]]
- Scale: 1,143,249 DASK policies in the 11 affected provinces; 326,895 damage notifications by early March 2023 (82% light damage); TRY 2.0 billion paid by then. [src: SBB [page 34]] DASK sells through 24 insurance companies and their agents. [src: https://tr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Do%C4%9Fal_Afet_Sigortalar%C4%B1_Kurumu (wiki)]

## Building codes

- Turkish seismic codes by year: 1940 (Italian code translated after the 1939 Erzincan earthquake); 1945 hazard map; 1949; 1953; 1968 (ABYYHY-1968, Afet Bölgelerinde Yapılacak Yapılar Hakkında Yönetmelik); 1975 (ABYYHY-1975, five-zone hazard map); 1998 (ABYYHY-1998); 2007 (DBYBHY-2007, Deprem Bölgelerinde Yapılacak Binalar Hakkında Yönetmelik); and the 2018 Türkiye Bina Deprem Yönetmeliği (TBDY). [src: https://tr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deprem_y%C3%B6netmeli%C4%9Fi (wiki)] This confirms the draft's 1975, 1998, 2007 and 2018.
  - The 1998 code detailed structural irregularities and introduced equivalent-load, mode-superposition and time-history methods. `[memory]` The draft's causal note "after Erzincan/Dinar era" is not in the source and is dropped as unconfirmed.
  - DBYBHY-2007 came in after the 1999 Gölcük earthquake; ready-mix concrete and the building inspection system became mandatory in the same period, and the 2007 code first set rules for assessing and strengthening existing buildings. [src: same page]
  - TBDY was published in the Resmî Gazete on 18 March 2018 (No. 30364 mükerrer) together with the new hazard map decision, and came into force on 1 January 2019. The new map drops the zone system and gives hazard by coordinates. [src: same page] SBB [page 24] gives the map decision as 2018/11275 and says the code covers design of new buildings and assessment and strengthening of existing ones.
- The 1999 earthquake drove reform: the SBB report says major legal changes followed the 1999 Marmara earthquake. [src: SBB [page 24]] These include Law 4708 on building inspection (2001; 19 pilot provinces; nationwide from 2011; amended 2018) and Law 6306 (2012) on transforming disaster-risk areas. [src: SBB [page 24]]
- `[memory]` That the applicable code is the one in force at permit application, not completion. No source read says so.
- `[memory]` That buildings permitted before 1999 are the main concern. (The sources say only that inadequate enforcement was a factor in the 2023 damage and that the >50,000 deaths were linked to it. [src: https://tr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deprem_y%C3%B6netmeli%C4%9Fi (wiki)])
- Amnesty: the Turkish Wikipedia states that before the 2018 general elections the government passed an amnesty law for buildings and contractors that had violated the building code, as previous governments had done. [src: https://tr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deprem_y%C3%B6netmeli%C4%9Fi (wiki)] This supports the draft's reported "2018 imar affı"; the exact legal name and date were not read.

## Household and workplace

- `[memory]` Family plan guidance (two meeting places, an out-of-area contact, numbers written down, practise yearly). Generic FEMA/Ready.gov style; not in any source read. Check https://www.ready.gov/plan.
- Bag/stockpile: a 72-hour (3-day) kit is the standard; the recommendation arises because relief organisations may need up to 72 hours to reach affected people. FEMA's three-day list: water at one US gallon per person per day for at least three days (drinking and sanitation), three days of non-perishable food, battery or hand-crank radio, extra batteries, phone chargers, cash, copies of important documents in a waterproof container. [src: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Survival_kit (wiki)] The draft's "about 4 L per person per day" is the same figure (one US gallon is about 3.8 L). Also FEMA "recommends that everyone have a three-day survival kit for their household". [src: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergency_management]
  - `[memory]` That AFAD itself says 72 hours. The AFAD pages read show the bag campaign but not its text.
  - `[memory]` That items expire (water and food rotate, batteries leak, medicines expire, children outgrow clothes). Neither kit article says this.
- Business continuity: a business impact analysis (BIA) determines recovery time objectives (RTO: acceptable time to restore a function) and recovery point objectives (RPO: acceptable latency of data not recovered, for example how many days of data may be lost). ISO 22301:2019 (replaced the 2012 edition) requires an organisation to define business continuity objectives, minimum acceptable service levels and the maximum tolerable period of disruption (MTPD). [src: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_continuity_planning] The draft's definitions are correct.
- Hospitals: the US Department of Health and Human Services addresses hospital preparedness for temperature, power for life support, and evacuation in extreme cases. [src: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergency_management] `[memory]` The Turkish "Hastane Afet ve Acil Durum Planı (HAP)" term and the surge-capacity and triage details.
- **Corrected (hospital damage):** the draft cited "Hatay State Hospital, Iskenderun". The SBB report gives the verifiable figure: 42 hospital buildings in the region were heavily or moderately damaged (27 Ministry of Health, 6 university, 9 private) and 94 lightly damaged. It names no hospital in the pages searched. [src: SBB [page 59]] The named hospital remains `[memory]`.

## Warning and evacuation

- Earthquake early warning: the P-wave travels faster than the damaging S-wave, so a system that detects the first can notify areas before the second arrives. ShakeAlert issues alerts "seconds before significant ground shaking". [src: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earthquake_early_warning_system] `[memory]` The named "blind zone" idea. Related, Türkiye's system has an unavoidable delay of roughly six seconds for offshore events without seafloor sensors. [src: same page]
- Operational systems: Japan (JMA, fully operational 1 October 2007, first nationwide system), Mexico (SASMEX, regional coverage, dates from 1993 and 2005), and the US West Coast (ShakeAlert; public alerting from 2018; statewide California rollout 17 October 2019). [src: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earthquake_early_warning_system]
- **Corrected (Türkiye):** the draft said Istanbul had early-warning systems "for years (after 1999)" and that a nationwide phone alert did not exist. The page read says Türkiye's EEW system is developed and operated by Boğaziçi University and Kandilli Observatory, is focused on the Marmara Region, and is one of the "opt-in systems" in public testing (as of January 2026, when the page's deployment list was current). It had its first significant real-world internal test in the October 2025 Marmara Sea earthquake, issuing the first alert 8.4 seconds after origin time. Separately, Google's Android Earthquake Alerts was extended to Türkiye in June 2021; in the 2023 earthquakes it underestimated the magnitude of the 7.8 event as 4.5–4.9, so few people got a strong alert. [src: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earthquake_early_warning_system (wiki)] `[memory]` The "after 1999" dating of the Istanbul system.
- `[memory]` Tsunami: warning centres issue bulletins in minutes to hours; near-field tsunamis may arrive in minutes so natural signs are the warning. (The SBB report only recommends building a tsunami early warning system. [src: SBB [page 51]])
- `[memory]` Floods and weather: MGM coloured warnings; 112 and cell broadcast as alert channels. Cell broadcast is named as a channel for the national EEW systems of China, Japan, Taiwan, South Korea and Israel. [src: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earthquake_early_warning_system]
- `[memory]` Cell broadcast versus SMS in congestion.
- Evacuation sequence (added): detection, decision, alarm, reaction, movement to an area of refuge or assembly station, transportation; the first four together are "pre-movement time". People often do not react at once to an alarm, and tend to use the escape route they know. [src: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergency_evacuation]

## Observations relevant to CTCP's premise

These are inferences about the design, not facts about the world, and are not counted above. The last bullet is corrected to match the sources.

- Preparedness is mostly dated items with expiry: water, medicine, batteries, DASK renewal, drill cycles, plan reviews. Each has a period, a last-checked time and a due time, and is silent until it lapses.
- Plans reference places and people that change (meeting point, assembly area, contact number). Validity depends on the world, not on the plan's date.
- Warnings are durations of seconds to minutes; response actions are ordered without clock time ("drop, then cover, then hold").
- One household sits inside many overlapping plans: family, school, workplace, building, municipality, province, each by a different author.
- A relative's plan can conflict with mine; each party holds private information (vulnerable person, medication) they might share only partly.
- During failure, clocks, network and power may be absent; plans must be readable and executable when the server is unreachable.
- Reunification and shift work show two time commitments colliding with no resolution until the event.
- Conditions on plans: "if the road is closed use the other" is conditional, and conditions may be unknowable in the moment.
- Regulation years (1975/1998/2007/2018; the 1999 earthquake prompted the reform but is not a code year) attach a fixed attribute to a building that outlives paperwork. Note: the draft listed "1999/2007/2018".
